# HealthCore Compliance Reference: HIPAA and UK GDPR Permissibility

## Purpose and provenance

This educational project reference supports HealthCore staff questions about permitted and prohibited handling of health information. It summarizes the official HHS and ICO sources listed below and the assistant restrictions in the supplied HealthCore CONTEXT. It is newly compiled project knowledge, not a claim that HealthCore previously issued or approved an internal policy with this title.

Sources consulted: 2026-09-27.

## 1. US clinics: HIPAA permitted uses and disclosures

HIPAA permits covered entities to use or disclose protected health information for treatment, payment, and healthcare operations, subject to applicable conditions and safeguards. This does not grant unrestricted access to or sharing of health information. Some information and uses, including certain uses of psychotherapy notes, have additional authorization requirements. Source: HHS, Summary of the HIPAA Privacy Rule, https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/privacy/laws-regulations/index.html; HHS, Uses and Disclosures for Treatment, Payment, and Health Care Operations, https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/privacy/guidance/disclosures-treatment-payment-health-care-operations/index.html.

A use or disclosure must be permitted or required by the Privacy Rule, or supported by a valid authorization where required. A general consent form does not replace a HIPAA authorization when an authorization is required. Source: HHS, Summary of the HIPAA Privacy Rule, https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/privacy/laws-regulations/index.html; HHS, Difference between consent and authorization, https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/faq/what-is-the-difference-between-consent-and-authorization/index.html.

The minimum necessary standard generally requires reasonable efforts to limit information used, requested, or disclosed to what is needed for the purpose. Exceptions include disclosures to, or requests by, healthcare providers for treatment. Do not describe this exception as removing all privacy safeguards or applying to every healthcare activity. Source: HHS, Summary of the HIPAA Privacy Rule, https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/privacy/laws-regulations/index.html.

**Relevant references:** HHS, *Summary of the HIPAA Privacy Rule*, sections on permitted uses and disclosures, authorized uses and disclosures, and minimum necessary; HHS guidance on treatment, payment, and healthcare operations. See sources H1-H3: https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/privacy/laws-regulations/index.html; https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/privacy/guidance/disclosures-treatment-payment-health-care-operations/index.html; https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/faq/what-is-the-difference-between-consent-and-authorization/index.html.

## 2. UK clinics: UK GDPR health information processing

Health information is special category personal data. Processing requires both a lawful basis under Article 6 and a separate condition under Article 9. An Article 6 basis alone does not authorize processing of health information. Source: ICO, What are the rules on special category data?, https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/lawful-basis/special-category-data/what-are-the-rules-on-special-category-data/.

The health or social care condition under Article 9(2)(h) may apply when its requirements are satisfied. These include the applicable basis in law and the professional confidentiality safeguards described in Article 9(3) and relevant UK law.

Explicit consent is one possible Article 9 condition. It is not the only route for lawful healthcare processing, and a consent form is not a substitute for identifying the applicable legal basis and condition.

Processing health information without an applicable Article 6 basis and Article 9 condition is not permitted. Satisfying these requirements does not remove the other obligations of UK data protection law. Source: ICO, What are the conditions for processing?, https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/lawful-basis/special-category-data/what-are-the-conditions-for-processing/.

**Relevant references:** ICO, *What are the rules on special category data?* and *What are the conditions for processing?*, including the health or social care condition. See sources U1-U2: https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/lawful-basis/special-category-data/what-are-the-rules-on-special-category-data/; https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/lawful-basis/special-category-data/what-are-the-conditions-for-processing/.

## 3. HealthCore assistant restrictions

The following restrictions come from the supplied HealthCore CONTEXT, sections 1-3:

- No patient identifier or PHI may appear in the assistant's events, logs, generated responses, or outputs, including hypothetical examples.
- Questions about identifiable patient cases must be refused with a request to rephrase without identifiable data.
- Details of active or under-investigation security breaches must not be revealed.
- Confidential vendor-specific BAA or DPA agreement content must not be revealed.

Permissions that apply to authorized clinical activities do not authorize the assistant to expose patient information. This reference contains no patient case examples or confidential agreement content.

## 4. Scope of this reference

This reference supports plain-language answers about the principles in sections 1 and 2. It does not establish which particular Article 6 basis HealthCore has selected for each processing activity, authorize a specific disclosure, or create additional company procedures.

Answers can cite this reference's relevant section together with its named HHS or ICO source. HHS and ICO guidance must be identified as regulatory guidance, not misrepresented as an existing internal HealthCore policy. If a question requires an internal rule absent from the available documents, the source does not support a definite company-specific answer.
