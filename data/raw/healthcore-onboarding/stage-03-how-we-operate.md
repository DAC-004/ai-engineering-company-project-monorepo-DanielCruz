# How we operate

Source: HealthCore CONTEXT.md department briefings; specialized onboarding briefing stage 3 (How we operate). Examples are generic. This document must not contain real or realistic patient identifiers, names, member IDs, or clinical charts.

## Patient journey

A typical encounter follows this order:

1. Booking
2. Visit
3. Clinical documentation
4. Billing
5. Follow-up

Corporate staff should be able to name that sequence. A booking that is never attended still occupies a clinic slot. Documentation that is incomplete later shows up as a billing problem. Follow-up that does not happen leaves the journey unfinished.

## Booking

Patients in the US currently book by phone. Patients in the UK currently call a front desk. There is no shared online booking system across the network. Corporate work that touches scheduling must not assume a single shared calendar already exists.

## Visit and documentation

Each clinic still operates with its own processes. US clinics and UK clinics use different electronic health record platforms, and those platforms do not speak to each other. Clinical staff spend about 35 minutes per day on documentation tasks. When a person moves between locations or across the US-UK boundary, history does not automatically follow.

## No-shows

The documented no-show rate across the network is 22 percent. That unused capacity is both a patient-experience failure and a financial loss of approximately 1.8 million dollars annually. There is no proactive outreach system today. Corporate hires should treat no-shows as an operational problem owned with Patient Experience, not as an anecdotal clinic complaint.

## Referrals

Specialist consultations are one of the service lines. A referral is part of the same journey: the booking and the follow-up still have to happen after the primary visit. Fragmented records make that hand-off slower.

## Billing denials

In the US, the documented claims denial rate is 14 percent, more than double a 5 to 8 percent industry average. Claims are submitted with inconsistent coding practices across locations. In the UK, billing is a mix of private pay and a small NHS contract, managed separately. Revenue Cycle cannot answer collection-rate questions without calling around. A denial is an operational problem in this journey, not a finance-only afterthought.

## Generic example (not a patient record)

If a booked morning slot is unused because the appointment was missed, the clinic cannot fill that slot after the fact. If documentation for that visit is incomplete, billing may later deny the claim. No name, no date of birth, no member identifier, and no clinic chart is used in this example.
